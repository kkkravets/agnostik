Phase II single-arm study of palbociclib and cetuximab in anti-EGFR naïve patients with KRAS/NRAS/BRAF wild-type, metastatic colorectal cancer

Jonathan D Sorah, Dominic T Moore, Matthew J Reilley, Mohamed E Salem, Tammy Triglianos, Autumn J McRee, Michael S Lee, Hanna K Sanoff, Ashwin S Somasundaram
The Oncologist

Abstract

Background

Standard therapy for patients with KRAS/NRAS/BRAF wild-type (WT), microsatellite stable (MSS), L-sided metastatic colorectal cancer (mCRC) often includes chemotherapy with a monoclonal antibody (mAb) that targets epidermal growth factor receptor (EGFR) such as cetuximab. Preclinical studies suggest the role of CDK4/6 activity downstream of EGFR as a possible resistance mechanism to anti-EGFR therapy. Therefore, we hypothesized that the addition of the CDK4/6 inhibitor palbociclib would increase the disease control rate of patients with anti-EGFR naïve KRAS/NRAS/BRAF-WT mCRC.

Methods

LCCC1717 was a phase II trial with a Simon two-stage design in which patients with KRAS/NRAS WT mCRC having progressed on at least two lines of therapy were given palbociclib and cetuximab. The primary end point was four-month disease control rate (DCR). Secondary endpoints were overall survival (OS) and progression-free survival (PFS). If six or more patients achieved disease control at four months, the study would continue enrollment, otherwise the study would be discontinued.

Results

A total of 12 subjects were enrolled, 11 of whom had left-sided colon cancer. The four-month DCR was 41.7% (95% CI 15% to 72%) with median OS at 13.9 months and median PFS of 5.3 months, which did not meet the pre-specified endpoint of efficacy. The treatment combination was well tolerated with the most common treatment-related toxicity being decreased lymphocyte and neutrophil counts.

Conclusion

While clinical benefit was observed with the combination, it did not meet its pre-specified endpoint for efficacy in patients with KRAS/NRAS/BRAF WT EGFR therapy-naïve mCRC patients. Clinicaltrials.gov Identifier: NCT03446157.

  • The combination of palbociclib and cetuximab did not meet its endpoint of improving 4-month disease control rate in patients with refractory KRAS/NRAS/BRAF wild type EGFR therapy naïve colorectal cancer, however, it was well tolerated and a few patients responded to therapy.

  • The study was closed early due to not meeting its pre-specified endpoint for efficacy (four-month disease control in six or more patients).

Discussion

Colorectal cancer is a common disease around the world as the third most diagnosed cancer and the second most diagnosed in females. Approximately 20% of patients with colorectal cancer (CRC) are diagnosed with metastatic disease and historically 5-year overall survival rates were around 12.9%,1 however, patients with metastatic CRC (mCRC) have experienced marked improvements in overall survival (OS) now approaching 3 years due to advances in novel therapeutics including chemotherapies and targeted therapies such as VEGF inhibitors and anti-EGFR antibodies.2 Among the standard of care therapies for patients with KRAS/NRAS/BRAF-WT mCRC is chemotherapy in combination with an anti-EGFR antibody such as cetuximab; patients with KRAS/NRAS/BRAF mutated mCRC do not respond to EGFR inhibitors alone so were not the focus of this study. Additionally, patients with R-sided colon cancer are unlikely to respond to EGFR inhibitors regardless of KRAS status.3,4 However, therapeutic resistance inevitably develops, usually through aberrant activation downstream of the EGFR signaling pathway from increased signaling in the MEK-ERK pathway and increased expression of cyclin D which drives cell cycle progression via cyclin-dependent kinase 4 and 6.5,6 Therefore, there is a strong rationale for the combination of a CDK 4/6 inhibitor with cetuximab in patients with KRAS/NRAS/BRAF-WT mCRC who are anti-EGFR naïve. We hypothesized that the addition of palbociclib to cetuximab in patients with refractory KRAS-WT mCRC would lead to clinically significant cancer control and tested this in both anti-EGFR therapy naïve patients (cohort A, reported here) and those with cancer control for at least four months on prior anti-EGFR therapy (cohort b, reported previously).7

In this phase II trial of palbociclib and cetuximab in patients with refractory KRAS/NRAS/BRAF-WT, EGFR therapy naïve mCRC, we found that the combination did not meet the study pre-specified endpoint for efficacy (four-month disease control in six or more patients, defined as stable disease or better response after four months of therapy) to warrant further investigation of this combination. Despite the study not meeting its endpoint for continuation (with only 5 patients achieving disease control at 4 months), 5 of 12 patients did achieve some degree of disease control with the most common response being stable disease. If the 2 patients who withdrew for reasons other than progression were excluded, the DCR at 4 months would have been 50% warranting further exploration of the combination. Palbociclib and cetuximab in combination appeared similarly effective to standard of care third-line treatment with trifluridine-tipiracil and bevacizumab per SUNLIGHT Trial achieving a PFS of 5.6 months. Another comparator is cetuximab monotherapy where a study of heavily pretreated patients with mCRC were given cetuximab monotherapy, the overall response rate was 10% and DCR was 34%.8 Regarding response, ORR of the study combination was 33.3%, an improvement from 6.1% with trifluridine-tipiracil and bevacizumab and 19.8% with cetuximab monotherapy in similar populations.9–11 Additionally, 2 patients had their best response as 70% reduction in size of tumor or greater. These data and the combination being well-tolerated suggest that there could be a population of patients who may benefit from palbociclib and cetuximab, specifically patients with higher disease burden in need of a rapid response.

It remains unclear if this combination is superior to anti-EGFR monotherapy based on these mixed results and small sample size; however, there is some signal for benefit in a subset of patients warranting further study. TK activity was evaluated as a possible biomarker of response extrapolating from a trial where TK activity was evaluated in patients with hormone receptor positive HER2 negative metastatic breast cancer starting first line treatment with palbociclib and fulvestrant showing that higher TK activity was associated shorter progression-free survival.12 In our study, there was no significant (P = .07) association between decreased TK activity and improved PFS, however, further studies could be conducted to better define the borderline significance.

The small sample size of our study limited our ability to make any conclusions as to which patients may benefit from the cetuximab and palbociclib. Additionally, 1 of 12 patients had R-sided colon cancer, where EGFR inhibitors are not used due to decreased efficacy in this population.13 Future studies could fully sequence ctDNA to determine if there are specific mutations along the MAPK pathway that could predict response or resistance to palbociclib and cetuximab. Using a ctDNA guided strategy, future studies could investigate whether addition of CDK 4/6 inhibitor to KRAS WT patients with multiple mutations along the MAPK pathway might mitigate resistance mechanisms.

Overall, the combination of palbociclib and cetuximab did not meet its end point of improving 4-month DCR in patients with refractory KRAS/NRAS/BRAF WT EGFR therapy naïve mCRC. The combination was well tolerated and future combinations with chemotherapy could be considered. Further investigation is needed to determine if any other predictive biomarkers, specifically whether any ctDNA-based assays might predict subsets of patients that may benefit from this therapy, possibly MAPK pathway alterations.
